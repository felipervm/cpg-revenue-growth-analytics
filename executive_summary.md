# Executive Summary — CPG Revenue Growth & Trade Promotion Diagnostic

## Situation
The simulated portfolio finished at **$25.87M** of revenue against a **$26.88M** annual plan, a **-3.8%** shortfall.

## What drove the gap
Portfolio decomposition indicates that the largest driver is **rate / price dilution (~$1.00M unfavorable)**, while volume is modestly unfavorable and mix is slightly favorable. This suggests the portfolio is not primarily suffering from a broad demand collapse; commercial price realization and promotional dilution require deeper review.

## Promotion finding
High promotional lift is not consistently translating into economic value. One deep-discount Energy Citrus 12-pack event delivered **+123% unit lift** but only **0.14x ROTI**, meaning incremental gross profit did not cover trade investment. A higher-value comparison event delivered **+89% lift** with **1.82x ROTI**.

## Pricing finding
The PR-CHO-4 scenario model tests price changes from -3% to +5% under a disclosed -0.9 elasticity assumption. In the simulated model, a **+3% price scenario** increases gross profit and places price approximately **0.5% above** the competitor benchmark, indicating a candidate for controlled price testing rather than broad-based repricing.

## Customer / channel finding
**Customer D** is the largest customer-level plan gap at roughly **8.0% below plan**. Convenience and Club are also farther below plan than Grocery, while maintaining relatively strong contribution margins.

## Recommended actions
1. Reallocate trade spend away from promotions with ROTI < 1.0x.
2. Protect and scale events with strong lift and ROTI > 1.5x.
3. Test +2% to +3% pricing on selected SKUs with competitive headroom.
4. Build a Customer D recovery plan with Sales, separating volume, distribution, rate and promotion effects.
5. Standardize post-event reviews around lift, incremental gross profit, trade spend, ROTI and post-event demand.
6. Replace simulated elasticity assumptions with observed price-response estimates before production pricing decisions.

## Limitations
All data is simulated. The case demonstrates analytical process and commercial reasoning rather than claiming real-world performance or causal inference.
